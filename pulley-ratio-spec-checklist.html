Pulley Ratio Spec Checklist

The pre-purchase verification system for cable machine buyers who refuse to guess.

Most cable machine buyers don't realize that pulley ratio affects every aspect of their purchase — from ceiling height requirements to how the weight actually feels during training. This checklist walks you through exactly what to measure, compare, and confirm before you spend a dollar. Complete each section in order, and you'll know more about your potential purchase than most salespeople.

Section 1: Space & Ceiling Requirements

Before you measure anything else, measure your space against the ratio:

  • [ ] Measure exact ceiling height at installation location (not just 'about 8 feet')

  • [ ] For 1:1 ratio systems: confirm ceiling height accommodates full stack travel + your height + overhead pressing clearance (typically need 9'+ ceilings)

  • [ ] For 2:1 ratio systems: confirm minimum 8' ceiling for most standing exercises

  • [ ] Measure floor footprint available — 1:1 systems often have taller, narrower frames; 2:1 systems may spread wider

  • [ ] Check for overhead obstructions: lights, fans, HVAC vents, garage door tracks

  • [ ] Confirm floor can handle total system weight + maximum user load (check spec sheet for combined weight)

💡Pro tip: A 2:1 ratio system in a low ceiling often beats a 1:1 system you can't fully extend. Measure twice, buy once.

Section 2: Budget Reality Check

Pulley ratio affects cost at every level — map your true budget:

  • [ ] Document your maximum all-in budget (equipment + shipping + installation + accessories)

  • [ ] For 1:1 ratio systems: budget for heavier weight stacks (you'll need the actual pounds you want to lift)

  • [ ] For 2:1 ratio systems: calculate effective resistance (a 200lb stack gives you 100lbs working weight)

  • [ ] Price out cable and pulley replacement costs for each system you're considering (wear items matter)

  • [ ] Factor in attachment compatibility — some systems require proprietary attachments at premium prices

  • [ ] If comparing across ratios: normalize to 'cost per pound of effective resistance' for true comparison

💡Watch out for: Spec sheets that list stack weight without clarifying ratio. A '400lb stack' on a 2:1 system is 200lbs of working resistance. Always ask.

Section 3: Spec Sheet Verification

These are the exact specs to find, compare, and verify before purchase:

  • [ ] Confirm pulley ratio is explicitly stated (if not listed, contact manufacturer — this is a red flag)

  • [ ] Document weight stack increments (5lb jumps on 2:1 = 2.5lb effective increments — great for progression)

  • [ ] Check cable rating: minimum 2,000lb tensile strength for serious training (Kraemer & Ratamess, ACSM, 2004)

  • [ ] Verify pulley material: sealed bearings > bushings for smooth, quiet operation and longevity

  • [ ] Confirm adjustable pulley positions and total range of motion for your height

  • [ ] Check frame gauge: 11-gauge or thicker steel for stability under load

  • [ ] Look for weight stack guide rod diameter: 25mm+ reduces friction and binding

💡Watch out for: 'Commercial-grade' claims without specs to back them up. Real commercial equipment lists exact steel gauge, cable rating, and bearing type.

Section 4: Training Application Audit

Match the ratio to how you actually train:

  • [ ] List your top 5 cable exercises by frequency

  • [ ] For strength-focused training (heavy rows, pulldowns): 1:1 ratio provides more direct loading and simpler progression

  • [ ] For hypertrophy and isolation work (flyes, curls, face pulls): 2:1 ratio offers smoother feel and finer weight increments

  • [ ] If you train explosive movements: 1:1 ratio responds faster to acceleration changes

  • [ ] If you prioritize time under tension: 2:1 ratio's mechanical advantage can enhance eccentric control

  • [ ] Check if system allows ratio changes or attachment swaps for versatility

💡Watch out for: Assuming one ratio is 'better.' Research on pulley mechanics shows both serve distinct training purposes (Signorile et al., Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research, 2002). Match the tool to your goal.

Section 5: Pre-Purchase Confirmation

Final verification before you commit:

  • [ ] Request actual user manual or assembly guide to review before purchase

  • [ ] Confirm warranty coverage: frame (lifetime ideal), cables (1+ year), pulleys (2+ years)

  • [ ] Verify return policy and who pays return shipping for large equipment

  • [ ] Check manufacturer reputation for parts availability 5+ years out

  • [ ] Read negative reviews specifically — what breaks, what frustrates, what gets returned

  • [ ] If possible, test the exact ratio type at a commercial gym before buying

  • [ ] Confirm delivery method: liftgate service, room of choice, or curb drop

💡Watch out for: Brands that don't stock replacement cables or pulleys. A great machine becomes a clothes rack if you can't service it.

Section 6: Installation Prep

Complete before delivery day:

  • [ ] Clear installation path — measure doorways, hallways, and stairwells against box dimensions

  • [ ] Confirm floor protection: 3/4" rubber mats minimum under heavy equipment

  • [ ] Have basic tools ready: wrenches, Allen keys, rubber mallet, level

  • [ ] Plan for 2+ people for assembly — most cable systems exceed safe solo lifting weight

  • [ ] Pre-identify wall stud locations if system requires anchoring

  • [ ] Schedule 3-4 hours minimum for assembly (most manufacturers underestimate)

  • [ ] Keep all hardware organized by bag/step — don't dump everything into one pile

💡Watch out for: Delivery services that won't bring equipment past the curb. Confirm 'threshold' or 'room of choice' delivery in writing before you pay.

Key Takeaways

Pulley ratio isn't a minor spec — it determines your ceiling requirements, your effective resistance, your progression options, and how the machine feels under load. This checklist ensures you've verified every critical factor before money changes hands. Complete each section, document your findings, and you'll make a purchase decision based on data instead of marketing. The best cable machine is the one that fits your space, matches your training, and comes from a company that will support it for years.
